FRONTIER JOINS THE WORLD -- AND THE WORLD BITES BACK

FMW opened its Summer tour in Sagamihara with a night that will be
talked about in Kanagawa for weeks. Atsushi Onita promised the fans a
company that would go where the big promotions will not, and by the end
of the night his own crew was lying in broken wood with a new American
enemy standing over them.

The rookie Eiji Ezaki gave the fans their first surprise of the night.
Akihito Ichihara grounded the high flyer early and worked the back, but
Ezaki kept coming off the ropes. When Ichihara went for his Boston crab,
Ezaki blocked it, turned him over and locked on the hold himself.
Ichihara gave up at the ten-minute mark, and the young man left the
ring to one of the warmest ovations of the evening. Rie Nakamura then
beat Yukie Nabeno with a bridging back suplex after eight hard minutes.

The upset of the night came in the different-style fight. Grigory
Verichev, the Soviet champion who came to Japan with a reputation, took
The Shooter down twice with judo throws and seemed to be in control.
But the masked fighter caught Verichev's arm on the mat, rolled through
and cranked an armbar. Verichev held out as long as he could, then had
to submit at 6:11. The Soviet camp sat stunned at ringside.

His countrymen picked up the slack. Boris Gogichashivili and Koba
Kurtanidze overpowered the Americans Mark Starr and Jimmy Backlund, and
Gogichashivili finished Backlund with a lifting atomic throw. In the
women's tag, Combat Toyoda and Megumi Kudo had their hands full with
the giant Svetlana Goundarenko, but Toyoda isolated Debbie Combs and
put her away with a powerbomb at 11:19.

Before intermission, Onita took the microphone. He told Sagamihara that
FMW would go to the towns the others forget and put on the fights they
